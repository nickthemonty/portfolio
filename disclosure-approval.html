Overview

Problem

Approvers used the same screens as the employees filling forms out.

What I did

Led research and design for a dedicated approver view.

Result

90% satisfaction after launch. Shipped on time.

Team · Time

2 designers, researcher, 2 PMs, 2 eng leads. 3 months.

The problem

One screen, two different jobs.

Employees answer ethics questions; approvers review and approve or send back. Both used one interface.

“When there are so many things to look at, they can lose sight of the most important things.”Interview participant

Research

Talk to approvers, then test the fix.

11 interviews across 5 customer organizations, plus a survey sent to 40 customers. When responses slowed over the holidays, I shifted to technical discovery with PMs and engineering to keep the sprint moving.

Finding 1 · Infrequent users get lost

Guide them in.

Finding

Many approvers review once or twice a year.

Fix

A tip modal they can turn off; respondent-only features removed.

Finding 2 · Context lives elsewhere

Bring context to the review.

Finding

Approvers left the platform to look things up.

Fix

A side panel with HR data and disclosure history.

Finding 3 · “Now what?”

Always show the next task.

Finding

After submitting, approvers didn’t know what was next.

Fix

Redirect to a prioritized task list.

The pivot · Text over charts

Accuracy beat visualization.

Chose

A dense, structured text layout.

Rejected

The planned data-viz dashboard.

Why

Approvers valued accuracy; the design system had no flexible charts.

Cost

Less at-a-glance summary; ~2 sprints saved.

User feedback · Positive, yet constructive

Testing shaped the final details.

Customers found the new view clearer and easier to use. Their concerns went straight into the final design.

From my manager

“Nick is a skilled and thorough researcher… His ability to uncover key user insights has contributed significantly to the success of our team’s projects.”John Langford · UX Manager, OneTrust

What I’d do differently

  1. Plan research around the calendar. The customer survey stalled over the December holidays, and I had to pivot to internal discovery. Next time I’d start recruiting earlier and line up backup participants
  2. Measure behavior, not just sentiment. 90% satisfaction shows approvers liked the view. Time to approve and fewer send-backs would show whether the bottleneck actually moved
